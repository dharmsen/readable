# References

Literature consulted for the `readable` skill's writing rules, and what each
one contributed. Newest additions at the bottom.

## Foundations of the skill

- **ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English** (ASD, specification).
  The origin of the core style rules: one idea per sentence, active voice,
  one term per concept, short paragraphs. The skill applies the writing
  rules, not the restricted dictionary.
  <https://www.asd-ste100.org/>

## Consulted for the density, storyline, and explanation rules (2026-10-01)

- Meister, I. G., Nicol, D., & Pimentel, T. — *"Revisiting the Uniform
  Information Density Hypothesis"* (EMNLP 2021).
  Empirical evidence that text is easier to process and rated more
  acceptable when information is distributed evenly across a sentence;
  density spikes are the problem, not length. Basis for "short is not the
  goal; even is" and the one-concept/two-numbers-per-sentence cap.
  <https://aclanthology.org/2021.emnlp-main.74> (preprint: arXiv:2109.11635)

- Levy, R., & Jaeger, T. F. (2007) — *"Speakers optimize information
  density through syntactic reduction."* Proceedings of EMNLP-CoNLL.
  Foundational statement of the UID hypothesis that Meister et al. test.
  (Accessed via secondary summaries; not fetched directly.)

- Williams, J. M., & Bizup, J. — *Style: Lessons in Clarity and Grace*
  (University of Chicago Press).
  Old-before-new information flow; subjects as characters, actions as
  verbs; clarity comes from reordering, not from cutting (concision is not
  compression). Basis for the sentence-level old-before-new rule and the
  section-linking ("do not open a section cold") rule.
  Summary consulted: <https://thegraduatewritingguy.com/style-and-clarity>

- Minto, B. — *The Pyramid Principle* (SCQA: Situation, Complication,
  Question, Answer).
  Basis for the opening sequence frame → problem → question → scope →
  core idea: the document is the answer to an explicitly stated question.
  Overviews consulted:
  <https://modelthinkers.com/mental-model/minto-pyramid-scqa>,
  <https://analytic-storytelling.com/scqa-what-is-it-how-does-it-work-and-how-can-it-help-me>

- "Effect of Three Types of Redundancy Reduction on Reading" — *Journal of
  Educational Psychology*, 68(5).
  Classic experiment: removing redundancy from text damages comprehension
  and reading rate. Supports keeping function words and connective
  sentences instead of compressing. (Found via DOI; full text not read.)
  <https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.68.5.649>

- Newcastle University Academic Skills Kit — *"Signposting"*.
  Guidance on guiding a reader through a document with small connective
  phrases; supports the section-link rule.
  <https://www.ncl.ac.uk/academic-skills-kit/writing/academic-writing/signposting>

- Pope, K. — *Open Technical Writing*, §1.3 "Signposting and Taxonomy"
  (LibreTexts).
  Signposting vocabulary for technical documents specifically.
  <https://human.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Composition/Technical_Composition/Open_Technical_Writing_-_An_Open-Access_Text_for_Instruction_in_Technical_and_Professional_Writing_(Pope)/01%3A_What_is_Technical_Writing/1.03%3A_Signposting_and_Taxonomy>

- Nielsen Norman Group — *"Legibility, Readability, and Comprehension"*.
  Plain-language guidance (short sentences, common words, active voice);
  supports the "explain only what the reader does not know" scoping.
  <https://www.nngroup.com/articles/legibility-readability-comprehension>

## Consulted but not used

- BCcampus *Technical Writing Essentials*, ch. 3.1 "Readability" — mostly
  document design (fonts, margins, line length); not applicable to the
  skill's prose rules.
  <https://pressbooks.bccampus.ca/technicalwriting/chapter/readability>
